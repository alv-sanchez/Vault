# Paper trail — deciphering the "Supplier-Owed" direction

> This captures **what I interpreted** from a stream-of-consciousness product note, and
> **every assumption** I made turning it into a design brief. Nothing here is built. The
> two companion artifacts (`proposal.md`, `mockup.html`) were
> generated by Fable agents from this interpretation.

## What was said (raw intent)
- When a supplier **owes money to the distributor**, want **transparency — a total**.
- **Include the fees for visibility**, show **all the lines**, **roll up the fee**.
- Sources include **Inventory Receipts** (on top of transfers and POs).
- On the **internal supplier program**, need a **great experience for the agreements** —
  for **freight charges AND taxes** and such — because there'll be instances where they
  **split it 50/50, or any percent**, per charge type. Example: supplier fronts **100%**
  on **pallet tax** and the **Alabama state-line tax**, but a **different %** on other taxes.
- The system needs **a place to see what a supplier will owe you across transfers and
  purchase orders (and receipts), CALCULATED rather than manually input.**
- **Billback lines should roll up.**

## Deciphered direction (one paragraph)
Evolve the freight-only, single-flat-% model into a **multi-charge-type Supplier-Owed
Recovery Program**: the Supplier Funding Agreement carries a **per-charge-type coverage %**
(freight, pallet tax, state-line tax, other taxes, receipt fees — any split), recoverable
amounts are captured as **itemized billback lines tagged by charge type**, and a
**calculated supplier-level rollup** shows the **total owed across transfers + POs +
receipts**, itemized with a grand total — plus an **LWC experience** to manage the
agreements and view what's owed.

## Assumptions (the paper trail)
- **A1 — Reuse, don't rebuild.** Keep `Billback__c` / `Billback_Line__c`; no new billback
  object. This mirrors the BMS-5161 "add a source, not a subsystem" principle.
- **A2 — Charge-type dimension.** Add `Charge_Type__c` to `Billback_Line__c`
  (Freight | Pallet Tax | State-Line Tax | Other Tax | Receipt Fee). Coverage resolves
  **per charge type**, not once per billback.
- **A3 — Per-type coverage lives in a child config object.** New
  `Supplier_Agreement_Term__c` (agreement → charge type → coverage % [+ optional state /
  brand scope]) so one agreement holds many splits. The existing `Coverage_Default__c`
  becomes the **fallback** when no matching term exists.
- **A4 — "16-40 / any percent" = arbitrary configurable splits**, not literal fixed values.
  The point is *per-charge-type, freely configurable* coverage.
- **A5 — Inventory Receipts are a third cost source**, structurally like transfers and POs
  (a billback line can point at a receipt record). Exact object API name to be confirmed by
  the architecture agent against the repo.
- **A6 — "internal supplier program LWC"** = an experience on the **Account (supplier)**
  record (or a dedicated tab), covering both agreement config and the owed rollup.
- **A7 — The rollup is CALCULATED** (roll-up summary where master-detail allows, else Apex),
  scoped to **open/accrued** billbacks, grouped by charge type. Never manually keyed.
- **A8 — Coverage cascade (extended):** agreement term (charge type [+ state/brand]) →
  account default → 100% cap; an explicit **0% → no billback** (distributor-funded).
- **A9 — Scope:** this is a **design proposal / direction**, not an implementation. No
  product code was changed. It would decompose into its own epic/stories.
- **A10 — Freight folds in** as simply `Charge_Type = Freight` — the shipped BMS-5161 path
  becomes one lane of the general program, not a special case.

## Things I deliberately did NOT assume (flag to PO)
- Whether taxes are captured on the transfer/PO/receipt already, or need new capture fields
  (the architecture agent checks `Total_Transfer_Tax__c` et al. and reports).
- Whether "internal supplier program" implies a net-new app/tab vs. living on Account.
- How this interacts with `Floor_Price__c` and the freight-vs-claim overlap audit.
