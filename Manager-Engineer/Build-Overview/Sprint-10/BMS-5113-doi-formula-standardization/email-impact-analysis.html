00 What I'm solving — the issue, the impact, the need

Before the email even matters, here's the underlying problem BMS-5113 exists to fix.

The issue

Days-on-Inventory had no single authoritative definition. It was derived from a day-over-day on-hand-decrease proxy — inferring "sales" from inventory dropping — rather than from actual sales. That proxy counts every reason on-hand falls (transfers, adjustments, shrinkage, corrections) as depletion, so the DOI figure drifts from reality and can't be reconciled against how the customer themselves computes it.

Why it matters (impact)

DOI is a decision number — it drives replenishment, ordering, and coverage calls. A wrong DOI means over-ordering (dead stock, tied-up cash) or under-ordering (stockouts, missed sales). And when Ohanafy's DOI doesn't match the customer's own spreadsheet, trust in the whole platform erodes — every downstream inventory report gets second-guessed.

The core problem in one line

An inventory metric that people act on was being computed from a proxy that doesn't equal sales — inconsistently, with no shared source of truth.

Who the user is

Day-to-day user

Inventory / replenishment planners & buyers at the distributor. They read DOI to decide what to reorder, how much, and when — so they are the ones a wrong number actually burns. They consume it through the native Days_of_Inventory report.

Customer stakeholders

Pete Teske (defined the formula; out this week) and Steve Sloane (copied in as the standing collaborator) on the customer side. They own the "does this match how we calculate DOI?" sign-off — the reconciliation gate.

Note: the formula comes from the customer's own practice, so "the user" and "the spec author" are the same people — which is why matching their number exactly is the whole point.

Why it's needed / what "solved" looks like

  • One authoritative calculation. Sourced from completed invoiced quantity (real sales), at item × warehouse grain — not an on-hand-decrease guess.
  • Computed once, read everywhere. A nightly batch stamps Current_DOI__c / DOI_Status__c so every surface shows the same number, not a per-report recompute.
  • Standard trailing windows. 30 / 60 / 90-day views (and per the email, potentially 10-day) so coverage can be read over the horizon the business cares about.
  • Reconcilable to the customer. The formula must match how they actually calculate DOI — which is exactly what the email pins down.

01 What the email actually says

Two things: (1) drop the "forecasted DOI" framing, and (2) here is the real formula.

"I think it'd be best not to draw too much from the past conversations about forecasted DOI… those statements were made before we had the chance to see the tool." — on scope: the trailing-history calculation is the ask, not a forecast engine
"We calculate DOI based off Units on hand / Units sold during a previous time period (10/30/60/90) × the number of days in the time period… (and the subsequent daily rate of sales)." — on formula: on-hand ÷ average daily rate of sale over a trailing window
DOI = Units on Hand ÷ Average Daily Rate of Sale Average Daily Rate of Sale = Units Sold in Window ÷ Days in Window  ·  Windows: 10 / 30 / 60 / 90 days

A distilled Excel example was attached to the email as the canonical spec artifact — that file is the source of truth for edge-case behavior (rounding, zero-sales, partial windows).

✓ Formula matches what's built

The sheet's =(A/B)*C is the same math as S_InventoryDOI.calculate() — on-hand over an invoiced-quantity daily rate. No rework to the core calculation. The customer's own spreadsheet is now the reconciliation reference the epic was missing a definition for.

⚠ 10-day window is real

It's row 1 of their own sheet — not illustrative. We ship 30/60/90. This is now a confirmed additive delta (a 10-day field mirroring the existing pattern), not an open question.

⚠ Edge cases still uncovered

The sheet has no zero-sales guard (B=0 → divide-by-zero), no rounding rule (D shows 33,333.33…, E is clean by luck), and is aggregate totals — no item × warehouse grain. These stay open; the sheet doesn't answer them.

DOH vs DOI — one label, one formula shown

The file is titled DOH DOI (Days-on-Hand vs Days-on-Inventory) but shows a single calculation. Confirm with Steve whether DOH and DOI are the same number here, or whether DOH is meant to use a different on-hand basis (e.g. sellable vs total).

04 Before vs After — the number actually moved

Same physical inventory, same fixture. Only the source of the sales rate changed — and the DOI it produces changed with it.

Before — on-hand-decrease proxy

DOI = 24

Sales rate inferred from any drop in on-hand — transfers, adjustments, shrinkage, corrections all counted as "depletion." That inflates the apparent daily rate, so DOI reads artificially short.

After — completed invoiced quantity

DOI = 48

Sales rate from actual completed invoiced sales only. Real throughput → true daily rate → DOI that reflects how long stock will genuinely last.

24 → 48  (×2 on the same fixture) DOI = On Hand ÷ Daily Rate · the proxy over-counted the rate, halving the runway the calc reported

Why this is the whole point

The old proxy told buyers a SKU would run out in 24 days when it truly had 48 — a signal that drives over-ordering (buy sooner/more than needed) and won't reconcile against Gulf's own numbers. The source swap is what makes the figure both correct and reconcilable.

Reference fixture from PR #513 (BMS-3742). Note: the "before" value is not a stored field — the old proxy code was replaced, so only the new (after) DOI is persisted/stamped. 24 → 48 is a dev-time side-by-side, not two live fields.

06 The biggest impact: forecast ambiguity is dead

✓ Design question resolved

The stall on this epic was "is future / forecasted DOI in scope for v1?" Pete answers it directly: don't draw from those conversations — the trailing-window historical calc is the ask, and the forecasting is what the existing tool already does. That means the built mechanism is the right v1, and no forecast engine needs to be built. You can proceed to BMS-4543 (mockups) on this basis without further PO input.

07 What's still open (after the email and the sheet)

Reconciliation gate

SESSION.md's one real blocker: a live before/after DOI reconciliation against real invoice/inventory data + PO sign-off. The email defines the formula; it does not supply the Gulf data or a call on representative synthetic data. Still gates BMS-3742 ready-for-review.

Edge-case rules

The sheet doesn't define them: zero-sales (B=0 → what does DOI show — blank, ∞, capped?), rounding (whole days? one decimal?), and partial windows (item with < window days of history). Needs a call from Steve.

DOH vs DOI semantics

File is titled DOH and DOI but shows one formula. Confirm they're the same number, or whether DOH uses a different on-hand basis (sellable vs total on hand).
