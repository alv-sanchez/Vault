Lesson 1 · Epic BMS-5161 — Supplier Freight Cost Billbacks

What you're actually solving

Before any code: the business hole this closes, in a sentence you can say to a PO, a warehouse lead, or Gulf's CFO without looking anything up.

The leak

Gulf moves a lot of beer. Two ways it pays freight:

  • Cross-state transfers — hauling product between its own warehouses in different states.
  • Inbound POs — bringing product in from a supplier.

By contract, a large share of that freight belongs to the supplier, not Gulf. But the trucking cost lands on Gulf's books, and there was no place to capture it and no path to bill it back. So Gulf quietly absorbed money it was owed. That's the leak.

One sentence Stop Gulf from absorbing freight costs that suppliers contractually owe — by capturing them and routing them into recovery.

What "solved" looks like

The move that made it small

The epic was written assuming Gulf had no billback infrastructure — its own description still says Billback__c "has only 6 fields" and there's "no freight-cost field." That snapshot was stale. A code audit found a mature billback pipeline (BMS-4141) had already shipped: a 14-field counterparty-generic ledger, line detail, supplier funding agreements, an idempotent recompute engine.

So the epic shrank from "build a recovery subsystem" to "add freight as a new source into rails that already exist." That reframe is the most important decision in the whole epic — it's why it's three small stories, not a quarter-long build.

Principle — you did this, name it Verify the premise against the code before you build. The ticket's stated "code reality" was wrong; the repo was the source of truth. Reuse-don't-rebuild only works if you check what already shipped first.
