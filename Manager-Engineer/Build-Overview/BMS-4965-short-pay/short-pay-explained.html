Short Pay · BMS-4965 · explained simply

When a store doesn't pay in full

One screen that catches it. Here's the whole thing, plain.

🍺 A Gulf truck delivers $500 of beer to a store. The owner hands the driver only $480. They're $20 short. That gap is a “short pay.” Now imagine that across hundreds of stops a week.

😖 Why it's a problem

  • That $20 isn't written down anywhere useful → the company quietly loses money it's owed.
  • In Alabama it's illegal to sell beer without collecting full payment — so a short pay there is a legal risk, not just lost cash.
  • Their old tracking tool got shut off, so today a manager hunts through the system by hand to find who owes what. Slow, and easy to miss.

🧍 Who it hurts

  • Finance / AR team — chasing missing money with messy data.
  • Managers — wasting time hunting shortfalls by hand.
  • The company — lost cash + legal exposure in Alabama.

💡 The solution — the Short-Pay Review Queue

One screen that lists every short pay in one place. A manager opens it and sees who's short, how much, how old, which warehouse, which state. And it's smart about it:
  • 🚨 Big or illegal ones get flagged (“escalated”); tiny routine ones stay quiet so they're not noise.
  • 🔁 Repeat offenders (stores that short you again and again) stand out.
  • ✅ A button to resolve it — issue a credit or mark it collected — so the books stay clean.
Think of it as one inbox for “people who didn't pay in full” that sorts itself by urgency.
