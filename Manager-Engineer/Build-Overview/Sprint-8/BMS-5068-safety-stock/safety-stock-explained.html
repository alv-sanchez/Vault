Safety Stock · BMS-5068 · explained simply

Keeping a smart cushion of stock

How a single setting change can make sure shelves never run dry.

🧃 Imagine you sell juice boxes at a lemonade stand. Your supplier sometimes shows up late, and big events spike your sales. So you decide to keep 5 extra days of juice boxes stashed in the back — just in case. That stash is your "safety stock." The number of days it covers is your Days of Inventory (DOI).

😖 Why it's a problem

  • Planners at Gulf carry a buffer in their heads — there's no place to write it down in the system. When someone goes on vacation, that knowledge walks out the door.
  • If the supplier is slow or a big promotion hits, the system doesn't know to order more — it just keeps filling orders the same way until shelves go empty.
  • By the time anyone notices a stockout, it's already too late. Planners are always reacting after the fact, not staying ahead of the problem.

🧍 Who benefits

  • Inventory / demand planners — get a safe, self-service way to say "give this SKU (or everything from this supplier) extra buffer right now," with a required reason on record — instead of guessing and carrying it in their head.
  • Warehouse / ops — replenishment automatically orders more to match the new buffer, so the extra stock actually shows up instead of the setting sitting inert.
  • Ops managers — get a real audit trail (who raised what, why, and when it expired) instead of tribal knowledge that walks out the door with the planner.
  • Sales & service teams — fewer "we're out" conversations when a promotion or supplier delay was actually anticipated.

💡 The solution, in one line

A temporary or permanent "safety stock override" that raises a product's or supplier's target Days-of-Inventory — and that raised target automatically flows into how much gets reordered. It comes with a full lifecycle (schedule it for later, let it auto-expire, get reminded before it lapses) and a screen to manage it and preview the cost before committing:
  • 📈 Raise the target → the system recommends ordering more, so you always have enough days of stock to cover the risk.
  • 🔧 Configurable, no code change — a planner creates an override with a reason; the math takes care of itself.
  • 🗓️ It runs itself — scheduled overrides activate automatically, temporary ones expire automatically, and the planner gets a heads-up notification before it lapses so nothing silently drops.
  • 👁️ See the cost before you commit — an impact preview shows the extra cases and estimated dollars of inventory a proposed buffer would add, before it's saved.
  • 🛡️ Graceful fallback — products that aren't configured yet continue to order the same way they always did. Nothing breaks.
Think of it as a dial the planner can turn that tells the system "stay at least X days ahead for this product" — with an off-switch that flips itself.

Days of Inventory (DOI) = how many days of stock you have before you run out. This build makes that buffer a real, auditable setting — raise it for a risky supplier or a seasonal spike, with a reason on record, and the system automatically orders more to match, reminds the planner before it lapses, and reverts on its own. No guessing, no scrambling after a stockout, no forgotten buffers.
