1. The problem & who it hurts

A short pay is when the amount paid ≠ the total due, discovered after the money has moved. The headline case is Publix paying by EFT for less than Gulf billed — but Gulf was explicit it's far broader: checks, DEX/DSD deliveries, net-30. In their words: "there is no such thing as a no-pay — that is a short pay."

2. Why it happens (root causes Gulf named)

flowchart TD SP[Short pay: amount paid ≠ total due] SP --> P[Pricing discrepancy
Gulf's system vs. retailer's] SP --> Q[Quantity / item change at delivery
broken case, didn't scan, wrong item] SP --> D[Customer takes a discount
at payment time — no driver in loop] SP --> E[EFT settles async
no in-person moment to reconcile] SP --> N[Net-30 / after goods transfer
delivery closed before payment lands] SP --> T[Driver training
voids whole invoice vs. cutting one line]
The key insight for the demo: fixing the invoice at delivery only cures the quantity/item class. Price mismatches, EFT timing, net-30, promos, and a discount the customer applies when paying all survive a perfect at-delivery invoice. Gulf said it themselves for EFT: "we can't stop it from happening… it only gets us by the time we're doing a bank reconciliation." → a residual short pay always slips through, so a back-office handling path is mandatory. That handling path is what this build delivers.

3. What Gulf actually wants

Three layers, in Gulf's priority order:

  1. Prevent at source (their ideal) — get the invoice right at delivery so what Publix EFTs later matches (driver/DEX reconciles qty & price; a Publix/DEX integration syncs in real time).
  2. Detect at the stop (safety net) — a handheld check-scan / payment-validation layer flags amount-paid ≠ total-due before the driver leaves.
  3. Handle the residual (mandatory fallback) — approval gate at the stop, reason codes, an AR review queue, escalation, collection, roll-to-next-invoice, write-off — because EFT shorts can never hit zero.

This build is a complete, shippable Layer 3 (+ the approval gate of Layer 2). Layers 1 and the check-scan half of 2 are integration/hardware work (see §7).

5. The demo run-through

All against org ohfy-val-shortPay-2, seeded with the BMS4965 SHOWCASE - Repeat Offender Co (FL) account (4 short pays, 3 escalated).

1Frame the pain

Open on the number: ~1,000 shorts on the books, never followed up; caught only at bank rec. "Today, you just accept it — there's no gate and no owner."

2Driver finalizes a short pay → approval gate

On the driver handheld view, finalize a stop paying short over threshold. Show that the driver cannot complete the stop — it routes into the approval chain and fires the push/bell. This is Gulf's "they cannot complete that stop until they've gotten approval."

3Approver acts (the consumer LWC you built)

Open Short-Pay Approvals. Show the pending item — note the ⚠ 3× repeat-offender badge telling the approver this is a chronic short-payer. Approve it. Show the decision lands in the durable history.

4It reaches AR automatically

Open Short-Pay Review Queue. The cleared short now sits here as an open row (the capture→queue hand-off). Filter by Escalated and by state. Point out the ⚠ N× badges — AR instantly sees who the repeat offenders are.

5Resolve it four ways

Open the resolve modal on a row. Walk the four Gulf-named outcomes: Post Credit, Mark Collected, Assign to Rep (show it creates the rep's collection task on their route — the "sales attorney"), Write Off. Resolve one and show it leaves the queue with an audit row.

6Repeat-offender & escalation story

On the showcase account, show the trailing-12-month short-pay count = 4 vs. escalation count = 3 — the distinction between "shorts often" and "shorts badly enough to escalate." This is the durable signal that survives resolution.
