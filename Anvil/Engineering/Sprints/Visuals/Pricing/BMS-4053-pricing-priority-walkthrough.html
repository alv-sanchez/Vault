BMS-4053Pricing Priority

How Each Pricing Layer Picks a Winner

The three layers — Frontline, Promotions, and the Volume tier sub-layer — and the exact code that establishes priority inside each. Plus an interactive replay so you can watch the resolution happen at any quantity. Companion to BMS-4053-pricing-layers-visual.html.

0The questions, answered first

Why is Frontline first?

A promotion is not a price — it's a discount (base − X). You can't subtract from nothing. Frontline answers "what does this account pay for this item at this warehouse before any deal?" — the floor every promo modifies. It's also the short-circuit: no base (NONE) ⇒ promo layer never runs ⇒ "Call for pricing".

What is "QD-only"?

QD = Quantity Discount (the Volume promo type). Only QD promotions carry Promotion_Tier__c rows, so the tier-selection step is exclusively a QD concern. A Post-off promo is a single flat discount and skips the tier sub-layer entirely.

"Base in hand"?

Not a field — it's the hand-off. After Layer 1 runs you're holding a concrete casePrice (e.g. $69). That resolved number is passed down to Layer 2 to be discounted. "Base in hand" = "Frontline has produced a base; proceed to promotions."

Post-off vs QD?

Post-off = one flat discount, applies at/above a single Min_Quantity (often 1), visible on the catalog card, label "Promo Price", tierId == null. QD = a curve of tiers, deeper discount as qty climbs, dormant until cart qty hits a tier, label "Volume Price", tierId set.

Draft eligibility — how do promos apply to a draft cart?

The Status≠Draft/Cancelled filter is on the Promotion__c record, not the cart/invoice. It means "only published promotions are considered" — a half-built promo won't leak to the storefront. The cart's own draft state never enters the check; pricing re-resolves live on every catalog/cart render.

3Status — what this is, and the future done-state

Resolver caveat (per today's read of the work). The promotion data model (Promotion__c, Promotion_Tier__c, the junction objects) is considered the landed, agreed-on piece. The resolver you're seeing — S_PriceResolver with the full five-stage waterfall — exists and works against the seeded data in this branch, but it should be treated as this ticket's working implementation, not necessarily the canonical future resolver. Expect the official "done state" of promotions to refine or replace parts of:
  • The winnow contract — priority semantics and the "deepest discount wins, no stacking" rule may be formalized differently (e.g. configurable stacking, exclusivity flags).
  • Cart-context quantity summing — how qty aggregates across lines is a likely point of revision.
  • The catalog qty-1 assumption — Volume is dormant on the grid by design today; the future UX may preview tier pricing on the card.
Bottom line: the layering model and priority order documented here are sound and code-true now; treat the specific resolver class as a fast-follow surface that will need to align with the promotions team's final design.
