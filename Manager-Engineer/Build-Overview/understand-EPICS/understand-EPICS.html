BMS-5068 Safety Stock Controls

BUILT · PR #442 · ohfy-val-4217

Inventory — Replenishment · phase: crawl

👤 User

Gulf inventory / demand planners — the people who decide how much stock to hold per SKU and supplier.

💥 Business impact

Supply disruptions, supplier lead-time swings and seasonal peaks force planners to temporarily hold more stock. Static reorder points can't flex — so they either stock out or over-hold. This protects service levels without touching system-wide defaults.

🛠️ Intended solution

Configurable safety-stock buffers: raise DOI targets by supplier or by SKU, with a resolution hierarchy (SKU > supplier > default), effective date ranges (temporary/permanent), a Scheduled → Active → Expired/Manually-Closed lifecycle, expiry notifications, a planning UI with impact preview, and audit reporting. Extends the existing override stack — doesn't rebuild it.

🚀 Next phase

Merge PR #442 (the whole epic is in it). Then wire supplier-tier buffers into the replenishment pick-face path — deliberately deferred, gated on the open Q4 product call (pick-face vs inbound-PO).

🧠 Remember it as: "Let a planner turn up the safety buffer for one supplier or SKU, for a set window, without breaking everyone else's settings."
The room: questions they'll ask (and your answers)
🛠️ Senior Engineer

"Are we forking the inventory model or extending it?"

Extending. Reuses SKU_Override__c, the nightly expiration batch, and the S_InventoryThresholds waterfall (decision D1: don't create a new object). New fields + a supplier tier on top.

📋 Associate PM

"What's actually shippable now vs later?"

Config + resolution + UI + audit ship now (PR #442). The one deferred piece is activating supplier buffers in the pick-face path — fenced off pending the Q4 decision, so it doesn't block the rest.

💼 CEO

"What does this save us?"

Fewer stockouts during disruptions (protected sales) and less dead cash tied up in over-holding — planners tune buffers surgically instead of moving the whole system.

📣 Marketing

"What's the one-liner for customers?"

"Set smarter safety stock — per supplier or SKU, for as long as you need, without disturbing your baseline."

BMS-4965 Short Pay Automation

BUILDING · PR #439 (Ph4)

AR-Finance / Delivery · phase: crawl

👤 User

Gulf delivery drivers (collect payment at the stop) and the AR / finance team that reconciles it.

💥 Business impact

Drivers regularly collect less than the invoice ("short pay"). Today it's a manual, error-prone exception process, and FL vs AL have different compliance rules. Money leaks and disputes drag.

🛠️ Intended solution

End-to-end automation: driver-side detection with reason coding → FL/AL compliance escalation → resolution, reporting & history. Now decomposed into phases (Ph1–6 + returns, EFT, AR-aging). Ph4 = a configurable driver finalize-stop approval gate.

🚀 Next phase

Land 5625 (Ph4 gate, PR #439). Then the detection + escalation core (Ph1–3) — most still Backlog. Direction is now unblocked (3 tickets cut).

🧠 Remember it as: "When a driver collects less than owed, the system catches it, codes why, escalates by state rules, and tracks it to resolution — instead of a spreadsheet."
The room: questions they'll ask (and your answers)
🛠️ Senior Engineer

"Why a configurable approval gate instead of hard-coded thresholds?"

FL and AL differ and rules change — the gate (Ph4) is data/CMDT-driven so compliance thresholds move without a deploy. Ph4b adds the stateful multi-layer approve/reject workflow.

📋 Associate PM

"This epic is huge — what's the critical path?"

Ph4 gate is in review now. The value core is Ph1 detection → Ph2 escalation → Ph3 resolution. EFT/AR-aging/returns are later add-ons, not blockers.

💼 CEO

"Is this revenue recovery or compliance?"

Both — recovers leaked cash at the point of delivery and keeps FL/AL collection compliant, with an audit trail for disputes.

📣 Marketing

"Customer story?"

"Never lose track of a short payment again — caught, coded, and closed automatically, by the rules of each state you operate in."

BMS-5070 Shift-End Workflow

BUILT · PR #431 (Ph1)

Warehouse — WMS · phase: crawl

👤 User

Gulf warehouse supervisors closing a shift; finance consumes the shrinkage data downstream.

💥 Business impact

Shifts close with no standard handoff — breakage noted informally, cycle counts untied to shifts, pass-down context lost between crews. Untracked breakage = invisible shrinkage and no shift-attributed data for finance.

🛠️ Intended solution

A structured shift-end checklist: breakage review (aggregating picker reports + bin counts), cycle-count integration, categorized pass-down notes, and a submission step that rolls shrinkage up to finance.

🚀 Next phase

Merge Ph1 (PR #431) — checklist + breakage review. Then Ph2 (cycle counts + pass-down notes) and Ph3 (submission + finance reporting).

🧠 Remember it as: "A checklist that makes every shift close the same way — breakage counted, counts tied to the shift, notes handed to the next crew, clean data to finance."
The room: questions they'll ask (and your answers)
🛠️ Senior Engineer

"Where does breakage data come from?"

Aggregated from picker self-reports + bin counts at shift-end (Ph1). The schema was spiked first (BMS-4077) so phases don't rework it.

📋 Associate PM

"Why phase it — why not one release?"

Ph1 (capture) delivers standalone value and de-risks the schema; cycle counts (Ph2) and finance roll-up (Ph3) layer on without blocking the demo-ready core.

💼 CEO

"Why should I care about shift handoffs?"

Shrinkage you can't attribute is money you can't recover. This turns invisible breakage into shift-level data finance can act on across all 5 warehouses.

📣 Marketing

"Customer story?"

"Close every shift with confidence — breakage, counts, and hand-off notes captured in one flow, feeding clean numbers to finance."

💥 Business impact

🛠️ Intended solution
