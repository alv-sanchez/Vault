Shift-End Workflow · BMS-5070 · explained simply

What happens when a warehouse shift ends?

One checklist that turns a messy handoff into a clean number — and keeps nothing hidden.

🏭 Imagine a school with three lunch periods. After each period the lunch crew is supposed to write down how many trays got broken. But nobody has a form, so most crews just shout it to the next crew or forget entirely. At the end of the day, the principal has no idea how many trays were lost — or which crew broke them.

A Gulf warehouse works the same way — except instead of trays, it's beer, wine, and spirits.

😖 Why it's a problem

  • Gulf runs day, night, and mid shifts across 5 warehouses (Milton FL even has a mid-shift). Each shift is a different crew.
  • During a shift, product gets dropped, spills, or arrives damaged. That's called breakage.
  • Today, breakage is either typed in manually at the end of a shift (and often skipped) or lost completely between crews.
  • When breakage isn't recorded, it turns into invisible shrinkage — product that vanished but nobody can explain where or when.
  • Finance closes the books on dirty numbers. Supervisors can't tell which shift (or picker) caused the loss.

🧍 Who it hurts

  • Warehouse supervisors — doing a handoff with no structured checklist; verbal context gets lost.
  • Incoming shift crews — walking into a warehouse with no written record of what happened last shift.
  • Finance / ops reporting — monthly shrinkage numbers are guesses, not facts.
  • The company — can't tie a loss to a specific shift or person, so nothing improves.

💡 The solution — the Shift-End Checklist

One screen that a supervisor opens at the end of every shift. It walks them through a short checklist:
  • 📋 Review breakage — see every item reported broken during this shift, confirm the count.
  • 🔢 Bin counts — spot-check key inventory positions so numbers stay honest.
  • 📝 Pass-down notes — write a quick note for the crew coming in next (coming in Phase 2).
  • ✅ Submit — one tap locks the shift record and rolls up a single shrinkage number tied to that shift (coming in Phase 3).
Think of it as a pilot's pre-departure checklist, but for closing a warehouse shift.
